What is the smarter 24-month decision: rent or purchase?

The ownership question.

The decision scenario

Consider a business preparing to fit out a corporate office. One proposal offers plants and planters as an upfront purchase, followed by a maintenance contract. Another offers a recurring rental arrangement with service included. Both appear to meet the design brief.

Which one is more economical? A comparison of first-month expenditure cannot answer that.

The overlooked cost

Ownership may bring assets onto the company's books while leaving maintenance and replacement risk with the facility team. Rental may offer a smoother recurring outlay, but the terms can vary substantially: replacement coverage, annual increases, damage, relocations, exit conditions and end-of-term asset treatment all matter.

A better comparison

Use a common evaluation period — for example, 24 months — and compare on the same inventory and service standard. Include installation, planters, routine visits, reasonably anticipated replacements, relocations, internal coordination and end-of-term obligations. Run a sensitivity check for changing office layouts or early contract exit.

The commercial insight

The cheapest option at purchase is not necessarily the lowest-cost operating model. Nor does rental always win. Better procurement makes the uncertainties visible so finance and facilities can choose the appropriate allocation of responsibility.

Questions to ask

Who owns the asset? Who replaces a declining plant? Can inventory be increased or reduced? What happens during a renovation? What would ending the agreement cost? Is service availability comparable under both proposals?
