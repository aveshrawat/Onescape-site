The cost of a first impression.

The business situation

A guest-facing property depends on a consistent standard of presentation. Planting near entrances, lobbies, lounges and shared facilities is part of the overall setting, yet horticulture operates alongside housekeeping, engineering, security and guest service.

OneScape has worked on indoor planting and recurring care within hospitality environments, where appearance and operating-hour constraints matter together.

What often gets overlooked

The cost of a plant is straightforward. The cost of a repeated complaint, rushed replacement, avoidable disruption or last-minute attention from a facility manager is less visible. Procurement specifications often concentrate on inventory and rates rather than the condition standard that needs to be maintained.

The approach

Select plants suited to real light and air-conditioning conditions, agree appropriate service windows, define inspection routines and clarify when replacement or relocation is needed. The maintenance plan should help avoid unnecessary reactive work while accommodating the property's guest-facing rhythm.

The procurement insight

Compare what each contract commits to maintaining, not just how often someone will visit. For premium hospitality, condition-based review and clear service responsibility can be more useful than a long list of routine tasks.

What this story demonstrates

A well-run planting programme works quietly in the background. It supports the guest environment without repeatedly becoming an urgent facilities issue.
