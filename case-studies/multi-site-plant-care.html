The hidden cost of inconsistency.

The business situation

A managed workspace operator works across several locations. To visitors, the greenery is part of one brand experience. Behind the scenes, the sites are not identical. Lighting, access hours, layouts, building management rules and the people coordinating day-to-day requests all vary.

OneScape's engagement involves indoor horticulture care across multiple operating locations. The commercial challenge is not just keeping plants healthy. It is making the service predictable enough that individual centre teams do not have to become horticulture managers.

What often gets overlooked

A contract can specify the same visit frequency in every city and still produce inconsistent results. If inventories differ, service records are unclear or replacement decisions are handled informally, the facilities team spends time reconciling small exceptions. Those hours rarely appear in the vendor's monthly rate.

The approach

A common scope establishes the baseline: service frequency, plant condition expectations, escalation ownership and replacement rules. Each site then has room for practical adjustments according to its environment. The operating model favours visible accountability over unnecessary reporting.

The emphasis is on one agreed understanding of what good looks like, with enough local judgement to keep the promise practical.

The procurement insight

When assessing a multi-site horticulture partner, compare the cost of coordination as well as the monthly service fee. Ask how the vendor will handle site additions, scope disputes, changing inventory, replacements and exceptions between visits.

What this story demonstrates

A multi-location horticulture programme is as much a service-design challenge as a gardening assignment. Standardisation has value when it reduces uncertainty rather than simply imposing the same checklist everywhere.
